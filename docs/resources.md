# Startup resources

An index of external material worth reading, organised by what you are trying to do. None of it was written here. Every entry credits whoever published it, and every link was checked before it went on the page.

This exists because a founder asking "how do I run a customer interview" does not need another explainer. They need the three good ones that already exist, and to know which is which.

## Where it came from

Two curated collections, merged and de-duplicated.

The larger one is the **startup resource list assembled by [Third Derivative](https://www.third-derivative.org/), [New Energy Nexus](https://www.newenergynexus.com/) and [RMI](https://rmi.org/)** — three climate organisations who put it together for the founders in their programmes. It is the reason this index has a climate and policy section at all, and the reason the material skews towards hard tech and energy rather than software.

The second is a university entrepreneurship reading list. Its resources appear here credited to their own publishers.

**Credit goes to the publisher, not the collector.** A Y Combinator essay is credited to Y Combinator, not to whoever put it on a list. Where a resource sits on a platform rather than a publication — a YouTube video, a Google Doc, a Medium post — no publisher is named, because the author cannot be recovered from the link alone and guessing would credit the wrong person.

## What was left out, and why

The source list held 179 unique links. Ninety-two are on these pages. Here is what happened to the rest, because a resource index that quietly drops half its contents is not one you can trust.

**Sixty-eight links pointed at two domains that no longer exist.** `playbooks.cyclotronroad.org` held 45 of them and `growth.500.co` held 23. Both fail DNS resolution outright — not a redirect, not a paywall, no server at all. Cyclotron Road's playbooks have a living successor at [Stanford Climate Ventures](https://stanfordclimateventures.org/playbooks), but the paths do not map across, so recovering them is a title-by-title job rather than a rewrite.

**Fifty entries were the same Google Slides deck**, one row per slide. Counting a fifty-slide deck as fifty resources would have inflated the collection by a third. It is one entry.

**Seven links returned a hard 404 or 410.** Five were Y Combinator library pages, which moved when YC restructured; the material still exists at new URLs. One of them is worth naming: the deal roadmapping template that Third Derivative wrote themselves now returns 410 Gone. It was the only resource in the collection that D3 authored rather than collected.

Everything remaining was reachable when this index was generated. A few sit behind bot protection and refuse automated requests while working perfectly for a person — those are included, because refusing a robot is not the same as being dead.

## Using it

Take what is useful. These are other people's pages and the only thing this index adds is the sorting.

Two habits worth carrying over from the rest of this repository. A link is not a citation: if something here changes how you run your business, save the page into `_inbox/` with the URL and the date so the claim can be traced later, the way [getting your material in](getting-material-in.md) describes. And a reading list goes stale the way any other document does. Sixty-eight dead links out of 179 is what four years of drift looks like on a list nobody re-checked.
